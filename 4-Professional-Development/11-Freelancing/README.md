# Module 11: Freelancing & Bug Bounties

Exchange your time and specialized skills for immediate income. This path allows for flexibility and high hourly rates once you establish a reputation.

---

## 💻 Freelancing Platforms

### General Marketplaces
- **Upwork**: The largest platform. Good for starting out, but high competition.
    - *Strategy*: Niche down (e.g., "AWS Lambda Optimization Expert" instead of "DevOps Engineer").
- **Fiverr**: Good for productized services.
    - *Idea*: "I will dockerize your Python application for $50".

### Vetted Networks (High Paying)
- **Toptal**: Claims top 3% of talent. rigorous screening.
- **A.Team**: Focuses on forming product teams for startups. High rates ($100+/hr).
- **Gun.io**: Great for senior engineers.

### Strategies for Success
1.  **Portfolio**: showcasing your GitHub and technical writing is crucial.
2.  **Client Reviews**: Over-deliver on your first few gigs to get 5-star ratings.
3.  **Communication**: Over-communicate with clients. Soft skills win contracts.

---

## 🐛 Bug Bounties

Earn money by finding security vulnerabilities in software.

### Platforms
- **HackerOne**: The industry standard. Huge variety of programs (Starbucks, GM, Nintendo).
- **Bugcrowd**: Focuses on crowdsourced security testing.
- **Synack**: Vetted elite crowd only. Guaranteed payouts for some tasks.

### Getting Started
1.  **Learn the Basics**: OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities.
2.  **DevOps Angle**: Look for misconfigured S3 buckets, exposed `.env` files, or weak CI/CD pipeline permissions.
3.  **Report Writing**: A clear, reproducible report is worth more than a complex exploit.

---

## ✍️ Technical Writing (Paid)

Many companies pay developers to write tutorials for their blogs.

- **DigitalOcean**: ~$300 per article. Focus on Linux, Cloud, and basic DevOps.
- **LogRocket**: ~$350 per article. Focus on frontend monitoring, but also React/JS performance.
- **CircleCI / TravisCI**: Often look for guest posts on CI/CD patterns.
- **Draft.dev**: An agency that hires writers to create content for tech companies.

**Why do this?**
- You get paid to learn.
- It builds your personal brand (SEO).
- It serves as a portfolio for consulting.
