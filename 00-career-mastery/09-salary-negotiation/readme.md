# 💰 Salary Negotiation & Job Search Strategy

In DevOps, you aren't just selling your time; you are selling **Risk Reduction** and **Operational Efficiency**. This guide ensures you are compensated for that value.

---

## 🔍 Phase 1: The Job Search Hunt

### 1. Identifying "High-Value" Roles
- **Target Keywords**: Site Reliability Engineer (SRE), Platform Engineer, Cloud Architect, DevSecOps Engineer.
- **The "Tech Stack" Audit**: Look for roles using **Terraform**, **Kubernetes**, and **Go/Python**. These typically pay 15-20% more than "System Admin" or "Generalist DevOps" roles.
- **Remote vs. Local**: Aim for companies in high-cost hubs (SF, NYC, London, Austin) even if you are remote, as their salary bands are higher.

### 2. The Application Pipeline
- **Quality over Quantity**: Don't spray-and-pray. Pick 5 companies a week, find the Hiring Manager on LinkedIn, and send a personalized note referencing a specific tool they use.
- **The Referral Hack**: 60% of DevOps jobs are filled through referrals. Use your network or reach out to engineers at the company to ask about their "Engineering Culture."

## 💵 Phase 2: Salary Negotiation (The Scripts)

### 1. The "Anchor" Rule: First HR Screening
**Never give a number first.** Information is power. The first HR call is where most candidates accidentally disqualify themselves or leave thousands of dollars on the table.

**The Strategy:** Redirect the question back to their budget.

- **Interviewer:** "What are your salary expectations for this role?"
- **You (The Script):** "I'm really excited about the technical challenges we discussed. As with any role, I'm more focused on finding the right fit and understanding the full scope of the responsibilities first. Could you share what the approved salary range is for this position?"
- **If they push:** "I've seen a wide range for SRE roles recently, so I’d rather discuss a full package once we see if there’s a match. What is the company's intended budget for this role?"

**Why this works:** It forces them to reveal their "ceiling" (the highest they can pay) before you reveal your "floor" (the lowest you'll take).

---

### 2. The "Operational Value" Argument
When they offer a number, negotiate based on the **ROI** (Return on Investment) you bring.
- **Script:** "I’m excited about the offer. However, given my experience in automating CI/CD pipelines which typically reduces time-to-market by 30%, I was expecting something closer to $[Target]. Is there flexibility here?"

### 3. Negotiating "Beyond the Base"
If the base salary is fixed, pivot to the **Total Compensation (TC)**:
- **Sign-on Bonus**: "Can we look at a $5k-$10k sign-on bonus to bridge the gap?"
- **Equity/Stock Options**: "I'm a big believer in the company's growth. Can we increase the RSU grant?"
- **Certifications & Learning**: "Will the company cover my CKA/AWS Professional exam fees and professional development?"

## ✅ The Career Exit/Entry Checklist

- [ ] **Market Research**: Checked levels.fyi and Glassdoor for current DevOps bands in your region.
- [ ] **The "Walk-Away" Number**: Defined the absolute minimum you will accept before the interview starts.
- [ ] **Portfolio Proof**: Does your 04-projects-showcase prove you can save the company money?
- [ ] **Benefits Review**: Calculated the value of health insurance, 401k matching, and PTO.
- [ ] **The Brag Sheet**: A list of metrics (e.g., "Reduced AWS spend by 15%") to use as leverage during the final call.
